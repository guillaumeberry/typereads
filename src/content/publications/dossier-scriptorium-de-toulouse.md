The book outlines the context leading up to the opening of the studio in order to provide an overview of the state of typographic design in France and abroad.

Through student testimonials, the book examines the humanistic pedagogy of Bernard Arin (1938–2019), who succeeded André Vernette in 1982 and served as director of the workshop—which had since become a private institution—until its closure in 2005.

“Dossier Scriptorium de Toulouse” also explores the concepts of lineage and transmission. By presenting contemporary typographic works and examining current educational practices in France, Switzerland, and Spain, it successfully demonstrates the impact of the Scriptorium de Toulouse’s educational approach.

Over the course of its forty-year history, the Scriptorium de Toulouse trained numerous type designers and calligraphers, including François Boltana, Rodolphe Giuglardo, Franck Jalleau, Claude Mediavilla, Thierry Puyfoulhoux, Kitty Sabatier, and, more recently, Xavier Dupré and Jérémie Hornus.

For the first time, this book—drawing on numerous previously unpublished documents from public and private archives—highlights the crucial yet little-known role of the Toulouse Scriptorium in the graphic design and typography revival initiative launched by Jack Lang, Minister of Culture, in 1982.
