intro: This site gathers publications about type design (obviously). The goal is to give people knowledge and resources on the practice of designing signs across cultures and languages.
faq:
  - question: Why this list?
    answer: |-
      Collecting publications is nothing new. But being able to build a collective knowledge base on any topic and share it with the world is a contemporary opportunity to be seized. I'm privileged to have collected a bunch of letter-related publications: books, magazines and even type specimens, starting in the late 90s. That was just a humble beginning.

      During my time at Practica Program, we shared a very interesting list of books and resources on type design: generic ones, specific ones, historical ones, and so forth. I started to gather those publications in a single document. Now is the time to make this collection grow, with other people contributing to it.
  - question: What kind of publications and topics can I find here?
    answer: |-
      The collection focuses on what helps people learn and improve their craft in researching, creating or distributing fonts. Covering everything about languages, scripts and written communication is not the goal (although it is a very tempting endeavour). Calligraphy should probably be there, lettering is a close enough practice, and there are probably a dozen other topics with room around type design. But let's keep the focus for now.

      As for the type of publications: books, magazines and teaching/learning material are the core content. We may expand to podcasts and videos. But as the name suggests, reading is the primary mode.
  - question: How can I help?
    answer: |-
      Do contribute. Do reach out about typos, incorrect information, broken links, etc. If I'm crumbling under 100+ submissions a day, I may cry for help with the curation.
  - question: Why so many Latin-script publications?
    answer: |-
      Because I'm biased. And because most of the written literature I've encountered was published by Western culture, leaving very little room for other cultures and writing systems. Learning a bit of Japanese, having an introduction to Hangul or discussing Cyrillic opened the right path.

      That is probably the most interesting opportunity with this project: showing the gaps, or revealing the publications that exist beyond Latin-based typography. Maybe we can inspire people to write books outside Latin type design, and show publishers there is an audience.
  - question: Features, content and what to expect?
    answer: |-
      The information on each publication is meant to help you dig the web or ask your favourite local bookstore for it. On the roadmap:

      - Language names shown in their own language (Français, 简体中文…) to help non-English readers.
      - Short descriptions for more publications.
      - Maybe topic tags, if a common tag structure can be found.

## Contribute to the list

You can [submit a publication](/submit). I review every submission before adding it to the list. Big up to you for helping this project 🙌

## Colophon: how and what?

The library is a static website built with [Astro](https://astro.build). Each publication is a small text file in a public [GitHub repository](https://github.com/guillaumeberry/typereads), edited through [Sveltia CMS](https://github.com/sveltia/sveltia-cms), and the site is hosted on [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com). Hosting costs nothing, and the pages are light: no tracking cookies, no ads.

The interim typefaces are Archivo and Archivo Narrow by Omnibus-Type (SIL Open Font License).
